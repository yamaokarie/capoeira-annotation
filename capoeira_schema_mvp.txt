CAPOEIRA JOGO ANNOTATION SCHEMA — MVP v2.0
===========================================

PURPOSE
-------
This schema captures moments in capoeira video that experts judge to be worth
learning from. The goal is to build a dataset that helps students identify
what makes a jogo dynamic, skillful, and worth studying — and later, to teach
an AI what constitutes good capoeira.

Each moment is marked by an expert with:
  - Timestamp (when it happens)
  - Free-form explanation (why it matters)
  - Optional tags (what kind of moment it is)

Over time, patterns in these expert judgments will reveal what we value in capoeira.
Eventually, an AI trained on these annotations can flag similar moments in new videos.


STRUCTURE
---------
VIDEO contains multiple MOMENTS.
Each MOMENT contains:
  - timestamp
  - expert's free-form explanation
  - optional tags (multi-select)


VIDEO
-----
- url           : YouTube or Vimeo link
- title         : display name (e.g., "Ferradura vs Gegê, Angola Style")
- style         : angola | regional | contemporary
- quality       : 1–5 (pedagogical usefulness for teaching)


MOMENT (Quick Capture)
----------------------
- video_id      : links to the video
- timestamp     : when the expert marked it (seconds)
- marked_by     : name of the expert or mestre
- why_text      : 2–3 sentences in the expert's voice — what does this moment
                  reveal about the game? Not mechanics, but rhythm, intention,
                  surprise, relationship. See writing guide below.
- tags          : optional multi-select from the tag list (zero to many)
- created_at    : auto-populated timestamp


TAGS (Multi-Select)
-------------------
Experts can apply any combination of these tags to describe what the moment is:

**Offer & Intent:**
  invitation   — opening the body to provoke a response; an offer that is also a trap
  threat       — declared possibility of attack, real or fake; forces response
  redirection  — unexpected pivot; broke expectation or pattern
  pressure     — closing space, limiting options, forcing reaction

**Quality & Style:**
  deceptive    — cunning, malícia; reading and outsmarting the other player
  skillful     — clean, precise, well-timed execution
  collaborative — playful, non-combative, mutual exploration
  playful      — lightness, humor, ease; jogo became play

Use as many tags as fit. A moment can be both "skillful" and "deceptive".


WRITING why_text
-----------------
Keep it to 2–3 sentences. Don't describe the movements — that's not your job.
Instead answer: What does this moment reveal about the game? What should
a student understand about capoeira that they wouldn't notice on their own?

Think in terms of:
  - Rhythm: What changed about the flow of the game?
  - Surprise: What didn't the other player expect?
  - Relationship: What does this show about how the players read each other?
  - Intention: What was each player trying to do or hide?

**Good example:**
"Ferradura opens the space with a hesitant cabeçada — enough to test Gegê's
attention but not commit. Gegê reads it as a feint and holds her ground.
This is malícia: not attacking, but reading whether the other player is
truly engaged."

**Bad example:**
"Ferradura performs a cabeçada with incomplete extension, descending into
a lower stance that creates an opening in the center line, which Gegê exploits
by transitioning to jogo baixo with minimal upper body exposure."

(The bad example uses sports-science language and describes mechanics, not meaning.)


LANGUAGE GUIDE
---------------
Use capoeira vocabulary freely. Here are terms that work well:

Movements (in Portuguese):
  ginga, au, esquiva, negativa, rolê, rasteira, cabeçada, bananeira,
  queda de rins, chizora, chamada, meia-lua, armada, martelo, benção,
  chapa, parafuso, au sem mão

Concepts:
  jogo           — the game, the dialogue between two players
  jogo baixo     — the low game, close to the floor
  malícia        — cunning, deception, reading the other player
  mandinga       — the magic and trickery in the game
  volta do mundo — circling the roda; used to reset or catch a breath
  roda           — the circle of players and musicians

Avoid sports-science language:
  DON'T use: "spatial limits", "traveling mass", "center line", "low plane",
  "inverted", "tracking the torso", "defensive base", "neutralizing pressure",
  "underpass", "execute", "transition"
  
  DO use: capoeira vocabulary and everyday language about rhythm, intention,
  and relationship.


ANNOTATION WORKFLOW
-------------------
PHASE 1 — CAPTURE

Expert watches video on phone or desktop.
Taps to freeze at a moment that catches their attention.
Writes 2–3 sentences: why is this worth learning?
Optionally picks tags that describe the moment.
Taps "Save".

→ Database fills with moments tagged by expert judgment.


PHASE 2 — PATTERN FINDING (Later)

After 20–30 moments are collected, read through the why_text entries.
Look for recurring themes, pairs, or relationships.
Use those patterns to inform future development.


PHASE 3 — AI TRAINING (Even Later)

AI reads all the moments + free-form explanations + tags.
Learns what makes a moment worth flagging.
When shown a new video, suggests timestamps + can draft explanations.
Expert reviews and refines.

| tags | Array/Multiselect | invitation, threat, redirection, pressure, deceptive, skillful, collaborative, playful |

KEY PRINCIPLE
-------------
The expert's judgment is the data. The free-form explanation is how we learn
what capoeira looks like through expert eyes. The tags are signals for
pattern-finding.

Don't force experts into a fixed structure. Let them speak in their voice.
Let patterns emerge from the data, not from a schema imposed before
the data exists.
